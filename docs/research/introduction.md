# Introduction

The proliferation of internet memes has made humor a ubiquitous form of online communication. In multicultural societies like India, these memes frequently employ code-mixing—blending English with native languages like Hindi (Hinglish). Understanding such memes requires not just linguistic translation, but also decoding the cultural nuances and interpreting the visual context.

Humor relies heavily on incongruity, cultural references, and wordplay. When presented in a multimodal format (image + text), the humor often arises from the juxtaposition of the visual elements with the code-mixed text. For instance, an image of a traditional Indian setting paired with a modern English punchline written in Hindi script creates a comedic effect that is lost if either modality is analyzed in isolation.

This research addresses the gap in current meme analysis techniques by proposing a multimodal, culturally-aware architecture. We explore how large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) can be utilized to better understand the nuances of code-mixed Hindi-English humor by providing them with relevant cultural context retrieved from external sources. The goal is to move beyond superficial text or image classification toward a deeper, more contextualized understanding of multimodal humor.
